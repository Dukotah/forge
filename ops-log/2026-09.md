### Why this guide / why now

The 2026-09-11 ops-log named "ComplyKit guide — GDPR privacy policy requirements for SaaS" as a top Task (a) candidate. ComplyKit was at 4 guides (tied with InvoiceQuick for fewest in the suite). All 4 existing guides address the privacy-law-for-website-operators cluster (do I need a policy, what to include, cookie consent, which laws apply). None address the SaaS-specific dimension: the controller/processor distinction, Data Processing Agreements, sub-processors, and international transfer safeguards (SCCs).

**Why this topic opens a new cluster:**

A developer who runs a SaaS product has fundamentally different GDPR obligations from a developer who runs a content website. The controller/processor distinction is the most commonly misunderstood gap: B2B SaaS companies process their customers' end-user data as data processors (GDPR Art. 28), not just as controllers. Generic privacy policy templates don't address this. The queries "gdpr saas privacy policy", "data processing agreement saas", "sub-processor list privacy policy", and "gdpr legitimate interest analytics" are all genuinely separate from the existing ComplyKit guide cluster — they target SaaS founders and engineering leads, not content site operators.

### Guide content summary

**JSON-LD:** TechArticle with nested FAQPage (7 questions) as mainEntity, datePublished/dateModified 2026-09-13

**Sections:**

1. **Your two GDPR roles** — visual controller/processor cards distinguishing: controller (your marketing and billing data, you decide the purpose) vs. processor (your customers' end-user data stored in the app, you act on their instructions). Both must be addressed.

2. **Data Processing Agreements (DPAs)** — GDPR Article 28 requirement; what a DPA must specify; why enterprise customers require one (procurement blockers, legal obligation on the controller side); practical approach (standard click-through DPA in account settings).

3. **Sub-processors** — definition and the GDPR Art. 28(2) authorization requirement; sub-processor table by category (infrastructure, error monitoring, customer support, email delivery, product analytics, payment, CDN, auth); the test for what qualifies as a sub-processor.
