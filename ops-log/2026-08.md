**Eyebrow:** "React / SPA guide" — sixth guide category alongside Remediation, HSTS, XFO, X-Content-Type-Options, and strict-dynamic. The React/SPA scenario is categorically distinct from all five existing guides:

- Guide 1 (CSP basics): server-rendered or generic site, building from scratch, report-only mode, nonces

**Article structure (TechArticle + FAQPage JSON-LD):**

1. **Why SPAs are harder for CSP** — three structural differences from server-rendered sites: static HTML shell (no per-request nonce injection), runtime CSS-in-JS style injection, development tooling that uses eval(). Sets up the problem before giving the policy.

2. **The three violations you'll hit immediately** — three "violation cards" styled in the existing red callout palette:
   - Inline bootstrap script (Vite module-preload polyfill) — `script-src 'self'` blocks it; console message shown
   - CSS-in-JS inline styles (styled-components, Emotion, MUI, Chakra) — `style-src 'self'` breaks the entire UI
   - eval() in dev and some libraries (React Fast Refresh, PDF.js, Monaco Editor) — `'unsafe-eval'` requirement

3. **Production starting policy** — the practical starting point for a Vite/CRA static deploy. Every directive explained in a table with "why this value" rationale: why `'unsafe-inline'` appears in `style-src` but not `script-src`; what `blob:` in `img-src` and `worker-src` is for; why `'unsafe-eval'` is deliberately absent.

4. **Handle the bundler's inline script** — three options:
   - Disable polyfill: `build.modulePreload: { polyfill: false }` in vite.config.ts (simplest for modern browsers)
   - Hash it: compute `SHA-256` of the exact inline script content, add as `'sha256-xxx'` in `script-src`; Vite output is deterministic so hash only changes with Vite upgrades
   - Vite 5 `cspNonce` plugin option: for server-rendered / edge-function setups only, not static CDN

   CRA: `INLINE_RUNTIME_CHUNK=false` env var to move runtime chunk to a separate file served from origin.

5. **Dev mode is different** — why `'unsafe-eval'` and `ws://localhost:*` are dev-only requirements, and the practical solution: set CSP only in production server config (Netlify _headers, Vercel headers, nginx); Vite dev server doesn't send CSP headers by default.

6. **CSS-in-JS nonces (the strict approach)** — when nonces are possible (SSR: Next.js, Remix), per-library nonce setup for styled-components (`StyleSheetManager nonce`), Emotion (`CacheProvider` + `createCache`), MUI (`createCache`), Chakra (`ChakraProvider`); honest note that this only works with SSR and that `'unsafe-inline'` in `style-src` is the correct and accepted choice for static deployments.

7. **Deployment snippets** — Netlify `_headers`, Vercel `vercel.json`, nginx `add_header`. All using the production starter policy.

8. **Inline cross-link to strict-dynamic guide** — in the Google Fonts/tag manager tip callout: "see the strict-dynamic guide for how to allow dynamically injected scripts without 'unsafe-inline' in script-src". Body-text link, not just footer/nav.

9. **FAQ (7 questions):** style-src 'unsafe-inline' vs XSS risk; React production and eval(); Vite inline script explanation; blob: in img-src; API origin in connect-src; nonces on static hosts; HardenCheck F grade recovery path.

**Article structure (all content genuine, accurate, not tax or legal advice — clearly disclaimed at top):**

- **Currency choice** — three options (your currency / client's currency / neutral USD) with the FX risk trade-off explained for each. Key callout: always use the three-letter ISO code (USD/EUR/GBP), not just the symbol — the dollar sign alone is ambiguous across multiple countries.

- **Required fields for international invoices** — a six-item checklist of what's additional vs. a domestic invoice: full country in both addresses; ISO currency code on every amount; full international bank details (IBAN, SWIFT/BIC, bank name and address); your VAT/GST number if registered; client's VAT number for EU reverse charge; applicable tax notice in Notes; and a payment deadline that accounts for 3–5 days of transfer time.

- **VAT and tax across borders** — four-row table covering the most common scenarios: EU-to-EU B2B (reverse charge, zero-rate, client's VAT number, note required); UK-to-EU/non-UK B2B (outside scope of UK VAT, no charge, note required); anywhere-to-US (no federal VAT, do not add US tax); AUS/NZ/CA to overseas B2B (export, GST/HST zero-rated). Each row specifies exactly what goes on the invoice. Explicit "not tax advice" disclaimer at the top; structural framework only.

- **Four payment method cards** — Wise (best for most freelancers: local bank details in 15+ currencies, transparent 0.3–1.5% fee, client sends domestic transfer); SEPA (best for EUR-to-EUR within Europe: same-day to 2 days, usually free); SWIFT (standard international, $15–80 total fees, OUR vs SHA vs BEN fee instruction explained); PayPal (available but expensive at 2.9%+ plus FX spread, best for small amounts only). The cost comparison is explicit enough that a reader choosing between methods can make an informed decision.

- **Payment terms and delays** — three practical points: allow 3–5 business days of transfer time in the payment deadline; FX risk accumulates on long payment terms (30–60 day terms on volatile pairs warrant a deposit or forward contract); late payment clauses differ by jurisdiction (UK statutory interest, EU Directive 2011/7/EU, US has no federal equivalent so the clause must be written explicitly in the contract or invoice).

- **Upfront deposit callout** — 25–50% deposit for new international clients is standard practice, reduces both FX and non-payment risk.

- **InvoiceQuick-specific tips** — five checklist items for using the tool for international invoices: currency selector, tax field (0% for reverse charge with label), Notes field (IBAN/SWIFT, reverse charge notice, VAT numbers, OUR fee instruction), client address country, your address country. The international invoicing guide fills the third, distinct search moment: a freelancer who has landed a client in another country and needs a concrete answer to "what's different about invoicing this person?"

Key differentiators vs. generic "international invoice" articles:

1. **The OUR vs SHA vs BEN fee instruction** — almost no article aimed at freelancers explains the three SWIFT fee instruction codes. Most say "make a wire transfer" and stop there. Whether you specify OUR matters: on a $2,000 invoice, SHA fees can reduce what you receive by $30–80. The guide makes this explicit so the reader knows what to ask for.

2. **Wise's local account details model** — most "international payment" guidance still defaults to SWIFT as the primary option. The guide correctly explains that Wise's model (local bank details in the client's currency, client makes a domestic transfer) is now the most practical and cost-effective option for most independent freelancers, with SWIFT as the fallback for clients who can't or won't use it.

3. **The four-row VAT table** — rather than giving a wall of qualifications, the guide gives a table with four rows (EU-to-EU, UK-to-overseas, anywhere-to-US, AUS/NZ/CA-to-overseas) and specifies exactly what appears on the invoice for each. This is the format a developer or freelancer can actually use when writing the invoice, not just a list of rules.

4. **FX risk on long payment terms** — this is the practical consideration that's almost always missing. Someone who invoices in USD on 60-day terms and has not thought about what a 5% currency move means to their take-home is often surprised at payment time. The guide gives three concrete mitigations (bill in your own currency, request a deposit, use a forward contract) rather than just noting the risk exists.

5. **Late payment across jurisdictions** — UK freelancers have statutory interest rights they may not know about; US-based freelancers billing US clients have nothing unless they wrote it in the contract. These are not symmetrical situations and the guide treats them as distinct.

InvoiceQuick's three guides now form a complete invoice lifecycle: create the invoice correctly → send it professionally → invoice clients in another country. All three cross-link to each other from header and footer nav.
